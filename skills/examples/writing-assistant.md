# Writing Assistant

Use this skill when helping draft or edit written content — emails, messages,
documents, announcements, or any text the user needs to send or publish.

## Principles

- **Match the user's voice** — read their previous messages to gauge tone.
  Don't make a casual person sound corporate, or a formal person sound chatty.
- **Concise by default** — most written communication is too long. Cut filler
  words, redundant phrases, and throat-clearing ("I just wanted to reach out
  to let you know that..."). Get to the point.
- **One idea per paragraph** — especially for emails. Short paragraphs are
  easier to scan on mobile and more likely to get a response.
- **Clear ask at the end** — if the message needs a response or action, make
  that explicit. Don't bury it in the middle.

## By type

### Emails
- Subject line should summarise the ask, not the topic
  ("Need approval on Q2 budget by Friday" not "Q2 Budget")
- Front-load the important information
- Keep under 150 words where possible
- If longer, use bullet points or bold key phrases

### Messages (Slack, Telegram, Discord)
- Even shorter than email — 1-3 sentences ideal
- Use threads for context, keep the main message tight
- Emoji and casual tone are fine if the user uses them

### Documents and reports
- Start with an executive summary or TLDR
- Use headings to break up sections
- Tables for comparisons, bullet points for lists
- End with clear next steps or recommendations

## What NOT to do

- Don't add corporate buzzwords ("synergy", "leverage", "circle back")
  unless the user specifically writes that way
- Don't change the user's meaning — clarify and tighten, but preserve intent
- Don't add unnecessary pleasantries ("Hope this email finds you well")
  unless the context calls for it
- Don't over-format — not everything needs headers and bullet points
